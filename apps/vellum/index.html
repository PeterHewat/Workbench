        Help
        
          
        
            
            
          
        
        
        
        
        
          
        About
        
          
          
            
        • 
              What this is — a drawing tool for people who want the SVG itself to be good: a
              small set of primitives, a live view of the markup, and an export with no editor
              leftovers in it. It runs entirely in your browser, works offline once loaded, and
              keeps your documents in this browser rather than on anyone's server.
            
          • 
            
        • 
              Why "Vellum" — vellum is prepared calfskin, the surface manuscripts were
              written and painted on for a thousand years, chosen because it was fine, stable and
              lasted. It was also thin enough to see through, so a scribe could lay it over an
              exemplar and trace: our tracing paper is its descendant. That is what this app does —
              a reference image underneath, your drawing on the sheet above it — and it is the other
              half of the name too, because SVG, like vellum, is meant to still be readable long
              after the thing that made it is gone.
            
          • 
          
        

          
        Tools
        
          
          
            
        • 
              Select (S) — the resting state, with no drawing tool chosen: click a shape to
              select it (thin strokes have a wider invisible click area, and whatever is under the
              cursor shows the outline it would get if you clicked — the whole group, when it is in
              one), drag to move, drag on empty space for a marquee. Shift-click adds to the
              selection. A shape only starts moving once the pointer has really travelled, so a
              click that selects never nudges anything.
            
          • 
            
        • 
              Select — the resting state, with no drawing tool chosen: tap a shape to select
              it (thin strokes have a wider invisible target), drag it to move it. A shape only
              starts moving once your finger has really travelled, so a tap that selects never
              nudges anything.
            
          • 
            
        • 
              Panning — hold Space and drag, or drag with the middle mouse button;
              the wheel zooms. There is no separate hand tool to switch to.
            
          • 
            
        • 
              Panning — drag on empty canvas with one finger, or with two fingers anywhere;
              two fingers also pinch to zoom. Hold still for a moment before dragging on empty
              canvas and the drag becomes a marquee selection instead. There is no hand tool to
              switch to.
            
          • 
            
        • 
              Pen (P) — click for corner points, click-drag for curves. Double-click or
              Enter finishes; click the start point to close. A path with no curves
              becomes a line, polyline or polygon automatically. Esc cancels.
            
          • 
            
        • 
              Pen — tap for corner points, drag for curves. The bar beside the path has the
              three things you need while drawing: take back the last point, close the path and
              finish, or just finish. Double-tapping finishes too. A path with no curves becomes a
              line, polyline or polygon automatically.
            
          • 
            
        • 
              Rectangle (R) / Ellipse (E) — drag, or click twice. Shift makes a
              square / circle.
            
          • 
            
        • 
              Rectangle / Ellipse — drag one out. To make it exactly square or circular, drag
              the diagonal handle at its bottom-right corner afterwards: that one keeps both sides
              equal, so no modifier key is needed.
            
          • 
            
        • 
              Text (T) — clicktap to place, then type where the text sits on the
              canvas.
            
          • 
          
        

          
        Editing shapes
        
          
          
            
        • 
              Resize — drag the blue handles; the diagonal one at the bottom-right keeps the
              shape square or circular. Rectangles also have a corner handle just inside the
              top-right corner; drag it to round the corners, hold Alt to set horizontal and vertical radii separately. A faint dashed line ties each of these extra handles to the corner it works from.
            
          • 
            
        • 
              Rotate — drag the pink handle above a selected shape, or put two fingers on it and twist. Shift snaps to 15°. Rectangles, ellipses
              and text keep their type and carry the angle, exported as
              transform="rotate()", so a rotated rectangle is still a rectangle with a
              width, a height and a corner radius. Paths and polylines have the rotation baked into
              their points, which loses nothing.
            
          • 
            
        • 
              Nudge — arrow keys move by 1 unit, Shift by 10.
            
          • 
            
        • 
              Exact numbers — X, Y, W, H and the angle are fields at the top of the shape's
              row in Primitives. Typing one is the way to be exact that a fingertip cannot manage.
            
          • 
            
        • 
              Order — the ▲ ▼ arrows on a Primitives row move a shape among its siblings.
              When it is already at the edge of its group the move widens to the group itself, so a
              group is reordered by pressing the arrow on the row at its edge; the arrow is dimmed
              only when there is genuinely nowhere left to go.
            
          • 
            
        • 
              Align — hold Alt while drawing or dragging to snap to points that
              line up horizontally or vertically with other shapes (this replaces grid snapping
              while held).
            
          • 
            
        • 
              Z-order — the ▲ ▼ buttons on the bar beside the selection, or on a row in
              Primitives and Reference images, move one step (with Shift: to the top or
              bottom).
              Ctrl+[ / ] does the same from the keyboard.
            
          • 
          
        

          
        Paths and curves
        
          
          
            
        • 
              Add a point — double-clickdouble-tap on a segment of the selected shape. Curves
              are split without changing their shape.
            
          • 
            
        • 
              Remove a point — click it, press Delete. Its neighbours are joined.
            
          • 
            
        • 
              Remove a point — tap it, then the bin in the bar that appears beside it. Its
              neighbours are joined.
            
          • 
            
        • 
              Smooth ↔ corner — double-clickdouble-tap an anchor. Lines, polylines and polygons
              turn into paths first.
            
          • 
            
        • 
              Curve handles — the two handles of a smooth point mirror each other.
              Hold Alt while dragging one to move it independently; release Alt to
                mirror again. A cusp is remembered until you drag a handle without Alt.To break the pairing, turn the point into a corner by double-tapping it.
            
          • 
            
        • 
              Join — select two open paths and use the join button on the bar beside them, or
              Join in the Primitives header
                (or press J): they are connected at their closest ends. Or drag an end point onto another path's
              end point.
            
          • 
            
        • 
              Close / open — the close / open button on the bar beside the shape, the
              Closed tick in its row, or drag an end point onto the other end point.
            
          • 
            
        • 
              Split — select a point and use the split button on the bar beside it
                (or press X). An open path becomes two paths; a closed one becomes one open path cut at that
              point.
            
          • 
          
        

          
        Style (Primitives list)
        
          
          
            
        • 
              Stroke / Fill — clicktap a swatch for the colour picker (hue, saturation,
              brightness, hex and alpha). The Fill checkbox turns fill on or off.
            
          • 
            
        • 
              Fill type — Solid, Linear Gradient (with Angle) or Radial Gradient; both
              gradient colors have their own alpha.
            
          • 
            
        • 
              Width, Line cap, Line join — stroke geometry. Width 0 exports as no stroke.
            
          • 
            
        • 
              Start / End — arrow, dot, square or diamond markers on lines, polylines and
              paths.
            
          • 
            
        • 
              Names — every shape gets a default name (path 1, rect 2, …) that stays with it
              when you move it; type in the row title to change it (clearing it restores a default).
              Exported as id="d8e4764a_name"; only the first underscore separates the
              generated id from your name.
            
          • 
            
        • 
              The colored square in each row shows the stroke as its border and the fill inside.
            
          • 
          
        

          
        Groups
        
          
          
            
        • 
              The group and ungroup buttons on the bar beside the selection, or in the Primitives
              header, or Ctrl+G and Ctrl+Shift+G. Selecting a member selects the whole group; the ◉ / ○ button in the list selects
              single members. Exported as <g>.
            
          • 
          
        

          
        Clipboard
        
          
          
            
        • 
              Ctrl+C / X / V copy, cut and paste shapes
              (offset each time). Paste also accepts SVG markup copied from anywhere, and works
              between browser tabs. Ctrl+D duplicates.
            
          • 
          
        

          
        History
        
          
          
            
        • 
              Undo / Redo buttons in the toolbar, or Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y.
            
          • 
          
        

          
        Document panel
        
          
          
            
        • 
              The Document button (first in the toolbar) toggles a panel docked on the left.
              It floats over the canvas and stays open while you work. Its sections (Documents,
              Reference images, SVG, Primitives) fold open and closed, and are remembered. The panel
              is one scrolling surface: only the SVG source keeps a scrollbar of its own. Every row
              across the lists is built the same way, and the control at its head says which kind of
              choice it is — a round radio where only one thing can be chosen (which document is
              open), a square checkbox where any number can (which shapes are selected, which
              overlays are shown).
            
          • 
            
        • 
              Reference images — add reference images to trace; each row is the file it came
              from, and clickingtapping
              that file name swaps in a different one. Open the row for position, scale, rotation
              and opacity; the dot beside it shows the overlay or hides it.
            
          • 
            
        • 
              SVG — the artboard size (width × height), the grid (its visibility and its step
              in artboard units), the background colour, and the live markup, which you can
              edit directly: changes apply to the drawing after a short pause (an error
              message appears if the markup is invalid, and nothing is changed until it is valid).
              Click or move the cursor into a shape's line and that shape is selected, exactly as if
              you had picked it in Primitives; selected shapes are shown in blue. When a field in a
              Primitives row has focus, the SVG attribute it edits is highlighted in yellow, even if
              the shape is not selected. Only shapes the app knows (path, line, rect, circle,
              ellipse, polyline, polygon, text) are kept. Icons import, export and copy the SVG
              (coordinates are exported as integers).
            
          • 
            
        • 
              Background — the swatch beside the grid paints the whole artboard, alpha
              included. At alpha 0 the document is transparent, shown on the canvas as a
              checkerboard and exported with no background at all; at anything above 0 it is
              exported as a full-artboard <rect id="background">, so what you see
              is what the file contains.
            
          • 
            
        • Primitives — one row per shape (see Style).
          • 
            
        • 
              Documents save automatically, like Pages or Notes on macOS — there is no Save button.
              Documents lists everything stored in this browser (reference images included).
              ClickTap a document to
              open it; clicktap the
              name of the open document to rename it in place. Every row reads like the other lists:
              a dot showing which one is open, then duplicate and delete (deleting an empty document
              doesn't ask; others do). + starts a new document (named Untitled, Untitled 2,
              …; the current one is already saved). The last document reopens when you start the
              app. Documents live in this browser only, so back them up: the ⬇ / ⬆ buttons in the
              Documents header export every document to one JSON file and restore them again
              (restored copies are added alongside your existing ones, never overwriting them).
                To empty a document, Ctrl+A then Delete.
            
          • 
          
        

          
        Toolbar: grid and view
        
          
          
            
        • 
              The magnet (or G) toggles snap to grid; a
              marker shows where the point will land. Snapping applies to drawing, dragged handles
              and moved shapes; hold Alt to switch to alignment snapping instead. Whether the grid is shown, and how far apart its lines are, belong to the document,
              so they live in the SVG section of the Document panel beside the artboard size.
            
          • 
            
        • 
              Fit view, zoom out / 100% / zoom in and the eye button (Final SVG: hides the
              grid, guides and reference images). On a narrow screen these fold behind one button
              near the right of the bar. ? stays out of that menu, last in the bar: the one
              button you reach for when you are lost should not itself be behind a menu.
            
          • 
            
        • 
              On a phone both bars float over the canvas rather than taking a strip of it, so the
              artboard keeps the screen. They start below the ruler, and the canvas between the
              buttons still pans — only the buttons themselves take the touch.
            
          • 
          
        

          
        The bar beside the selection
        
          
          
            
        • 
              Select a shape and a small bar appears next to it with what applies: delete,
              duplicate, send backward / bring forward (dimmed when there is nowhere to go), close
              or open a path, join two paths, group and ungroup, and edit text. It is the same set
              the keyboard shortcuts reach, within reach of a thumb.
            
          • 
            
        • 
              Select one point of a path and the bar narrows to that point: split the path
              here, close or open it, delete this point. The whole-shape actions step aside, because
              beside a highlighted point they would act on something other than what is highlighted
              — delete most of all.
            
          • 
            
        • 
              While the pen has a path open the bar shows that instead: take back the last point,
              close the path and finish, or just finish. That is how you end a path without a
              keyboard.
            
          • 
          
        

          
        Rulers
        
          
          
            
        • 
              Rulers along the top and left show artboard units and a cursor marker. When the
              Document panel is open they start to its right.
            
          • 
          
        

          
        Touch
        
          
          
            
        • 
              Handles get a finger-sized target on a touch screen, and points that sit close
              together keep smaller ones so neighbours stay reachable. Zoom in where it is tight.
            
          • 
            
        • 
              Any tool can grab an existing handle: with the rectangle or ellipse tool active,
              touching a handle of a shape already on the canvas edits that shape rather than
              starting a new one.
