          About
          
          
            
            
          • 
              Vellum is part of Workbench, a set of useful tools that run entirely
              in your browser and keep working offline.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              What this is — a drawing tool for people who want the SVG itself to be good: a
              small set of primitives, a live view of the markup, and an export with no editor
              leftovers in it. It runs entirely in your browser, works offline once loaded, and
              keeps your documents in this browser rather than on anyone's server.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Why "Vellum" — vellum is prepared calfskin, the surface manuscripts were
              written and painted on for a thousand years, chosen because it was fine, stable and
              lasted. It was also thin enough to see through, so a scribe could lay it over an
              exemplar and trace: our tracing paper is its descendant. That is what this app does —
              a reference image underneath, your drawing on the sheet above it — and it is the other
              half of the name too, because SVG, like vellum, is meant to still be readable long
              after the thing that made it is gone.
            
            • 
          
          

          
          Tools
          
          
            
            
          • 
              Select (S) — the resting state, with no drawing tool chosen: click a shape to
              select it (thin strokes have a wider invisible click area, and whatever is under the
              cursor shows the outline it would get if you clicked — the whole group, when it is in
              one), drag to move, drag on empty space for a marquee. Shift-click adds to the
              selection. A shape only starts moving once the pointer has really travelled, so a
              click that selects never nudges anything.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Select — the resting state, with no drawing tool chosen: tap a shape to select
              it (thin strokes have a wider invisible target), drag it to move it. A shape only
              starts moving once your finger has really travelled, so a tap that selects never
              nudges anything. To select several, turn on add to the selection (the arrow
              with a plus, first on the bar beside the selection): while it is on, a tap adds a
              shape or takes it out, as Shift-click does with a mouse. It lets go by itself once
              nothing is selected. Select everything is on the same bar.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Panning — hold Space and drag, or drag with the middle mouse button;
              the wheel zooms. There is no separate hand tool to switch to.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Panning — drag on empty canvas with one finger, or with two fingers anywhere;
              two fingers also pinch to zoom. Hold still for a moment before dragging on empty
              canvas and the drag becomes a marquee selection instead. There is no hand tool to
              switch to.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Pen (P) — click for corner points, click-drag for curves. Double-click or
              Enter finishes; click the start point to close — from the second point on,
              and the start point is ringed while a click would close it. A path with no curves
              becomes a line, polyline or polygon automatically. Esc cancels.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Pen — tap for corner points, drag for curves. The bar beside the path has what
              you need while drawing: take back the last point, close the path and finish, just
              finish, or throw the path away. Double-tapping finishes too. A path with no curves
              becomes a line, polyline or polygon automatically.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Rectangle (R) / Ellipse (E) — drag, or click twice. Shift makes a
              square / circle.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Rectangle / Ellipse — drag one out. To make it exactly square or circular, drag
              the diagonal handle at its bottom-right corner afterwards: that one keeps both sides
              equal, so no modifier key is needed.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Text (T) — clicktap to place, then type where the text sits on the
              canvas.
            
            • 
          
          

          
          Editing shapes
          
          
            
            
          • 
              Resize — drag the blue handles; the diagonal one at the bottom-right keeps the
              shape square or circular. Rectangles also have a corner handle just inside the
              top-right corner; drag it to round the corners, hold Alt to set horizontal and vertical radii separately. A faint dashed line ties each of these extra handles to the corner it works from. A
              path, polyline or polygon has a hollow handle just off each corner of its box: drag
              one to stretch the shape from the opposite corner, with Shift to keep its proportions.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Rotate — drag the pink handle above a selected shape, or put two fingers on it and twist. Shift snaps to 15°.
                The angle catches on each 15° as it passes and lets go as you keep turning.
              Rectangles, ellipses and text keep their type and carry the angle, exported as
              transform="rotate()", so a rotated rectangle is still a rectangle with a
              width, a height and a corner radius. Paths and polylines have the rotation baked into
              their points, which loses nothing.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Nudge — arrow keys move the selection by 1 unit, Shift by 10. With a
              point picked they move only that point, or the curve handle you grabbed last.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Align and distribute — the align button on the bar opens a page of six ways to
              line things up (left, centre, right, top, middle, bottom) and, with three or more, two
              ways to space them evenly. Several shapes line up with the box they share; a group
              selected whole moves as one; a single shape, or a single group, lines up with the
              artboard, which is how an icon is centred. Picked points line up and spread the same
              way.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Several points — with a point picked,
              Shift-clickturn on add to the selection and tap more points of
              the selected shapes, or drag a box round them. Dragging one of them moves them all,
              and so do the arrow keys; delete removes them, and the bar makes them all curves or
              all corners. A plain clicktap on one of them picks it alone again.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Exact numbers — X, Y, W, H and the angle are fields at the top of the shape's
              row in Primitives. Typing one is the way to be exact that a fingertip cannot manage.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Order — the Primitives list reads like a layers panel: the shape drawn on top
              is at the top of the list. ▲ brings a shape forward and ▼ sends it back, among its
              siblings and never out of its group: at the edge of the group the arrow is dimmed. To
              move the whole group, use the arrows on the group's own row. The SVG source keeps the
              file's order, where the shape drawn last is written last.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Align —
              hold Alt while drawing or dragging, or turn
              on the snap-to-shapes switch beside the grid-snap one, to snap to points that line up
              horizontally or vertically with other shapes - their corners and points, the middle of
              each of their segments, and the places where two of them cross. The two switches are
              one choice: turning one on turns the other off, and holding Alt borrows snap to shapes for as long as it is held.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Z-order — the ▲ ▼ buttons on the bar beside the selection, or on a row in
              Primitives and Reference images, bring forward or send back one step (with
              Shift: all the way to the front or the back).
              Ctrl+[ / ] does the same from the keyboard.
            
            • 
          
          

          
          Paths and curves
          
          
            
            
          • 
              Add a point — double-clickdouble-tap on a segment of the selected shape. Curves
              are split without changing their shape.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Remove a point — click it, press Delete. Its neighbours are joined.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Remove a point — tap it, then the bin in the bar that appears beside it. Its
              neighbours are joined.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Large selections — with many paths selected at once, their points are hidden
              and only the boxes show, which keeps moving them smooth. Select fewer shapes, or one,
              to work on points.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Curve ↔ corner — select a point and use the curve / corner button on the bar
              beside it, or double-clickdouble-tap the point. A curve gets two handles; a
              corner has none. Lines, polylines and polygons turn into paths first.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Curve handles — the two handles of a smooth point are linked: drag one and the
              other mirrors it. Grabbing a handle selects its point, and the bar beside the point
              breaks the pair into a cusp, whose handles then move on their own (their arms
              are drawn dashed), or links it again, mirroring one handle off the other. To take one
              handle off, leaving the curve on the other side, grab it and use the remove button on
              the bar, press Delete, or double-clickdouble-tap it.
              Holding Alt while you drag breaks the pair on the fly; let go of Alt
                before the mouse and it mirrors again.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Join — select two open paths and use the join button on the bar beside them, or
              Join in the Primitives header
                (or press J): they are connected at their closest ends. Or drag an end point onto another path's
              end point: a green ring round it shows that letting go there joins them.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Close / open — the close / open button on the bar beside the shape, the
              Closed tick in its row, or drag an end point onto the other end point (ringed
              in green when letting go will close it).
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Split — select a point and use the split button on the bar beside it
                (or press X). An open path becomes two paths; a closed one becomes one open path cut at that
              point. The two ends of an open path have nothing to split, so they do not offer it.
            
            • 
          
          

          
          Style (Primitives list)
          
          
            
            
          • 
              Stroke / Fill — clicktap a swatch for the colour picker (hue, saturation,
              brightness, hex and alpha). With a reference image in the document, its dropper picks
              a colour from the image: press it, then clicktap the image where the colour is. The Fill checkbox
              turns fill on or off.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Dash — lengths of dash and gap along the stroke, such as 6 4, in
              artboard units; empty for a solid line. Exported as stroke-dasharray.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Fill type — Solid, Linear Gradient or Radial Gradient. A gradient has as many
              stops as you add, each with its own colour, alpha and position, and two handles on the
              shape set where it runs.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Width, Line cap, Line join — stroke geometry. Width 0 exports as no stroke.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Start / End — arrow, dot, square or diamond markers on lines, polylines and
              paths.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Names — every shape gets a default name (path 1, rect 2, …) that stays with it
              when you move it; type in the row title to change it (clearing it restores a default).
              Exported as id="d8e4764a_name"; only the first underscore separates the
              generated id from your name.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              The colored square in each row shows the stroke as its border and the fill inside.
            
            • 
          
          

          
          Groups
          
          
            
            
          • 
              The group and ungroup buttons on the bar beside the selection, or in the Primitives
              header, or Ctrl+G and Ctrl+Shift+G. Selecting a member selects the whole group; clicktap it again to step inside, to the group nested there
              or to the shape itself, which then moves, duplicates or groups on its own and stays in
              its group. Esc steps back out one level. The
              checkbox on a row in the list selects single members too. Exported as
              <g>.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Move, stretch and turn a group — with a group, or several shapes, selected, a
              hollow handle stands off each corner of their shared box and a pink one above it: drag
              a corner to stretch them all from the opposite corner
                (Shift keeps the proportions), or the pink handle to turn them all about the middle. In the Primitives list an
              open group shows the same as X, Y, W, H and Turn by under its name, as an open
              shape shows its fields. The group keeps no transform of its own: every change is
              written into its shapes, so the angle is a turn by so many degrees, a rotated
              rectangle or ellipse stretched off its own axes becomes a path, and stroke widths stay
              as they are.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Combine — with two or more shapes selected (paths, polygons, rectangles,
              ellipses), the combine button on the bar opens four ways to make one shape of them:
              union keeps everything they cover, subtract takes the others from the
              backmost, intersect keeps only where they all overlap, and exclude keeps
              everything but the overlap. Curves stay curves. The result takes the backmost shape's
              place, name and style. A hole is a second outline in the same path: a path can hold
              several, and its Fill rule says how overlapping ones fill.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Merge puts the selection into one group without adding a level
                (Ctrl+Alt+G): select a group and a new shape to add the shape to it, or several groups to make
              them one. Groups nested inside them stay as they are, and the merged group keeps the
              name of the backmost named one. Group, by contrast, wraps the selection in a new group
              around it.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              In the Primitives list a group is one coloured line down the left of the rows it
              holds, and a group inside a group is a line inside a line. Each group gets a colour of
              its own when it is made and keeps it wherever it moves. Members stay together: the ▲ ▼
              arrows reorder a shape among its siblings and stop at the edge of its group.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              The group's row — a group has a row of its own at the top of its bracket, with
              the same buttons as a shape's: the chevron folds the group away, the checkbox selects
              all of it, the eye shows or hides every shape in it, ▲ ▼ move it as one block, and the
              bin deletes it with everything inside. Where a shape shows its colours, a group shows
              how many shapes it holds.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Naming a group — type in its row. A named group exports as
              <g id="group-57cc1c37_top_view">: its id, then the name, with
              spaces as underscores.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              A group holds at least two things. A group carries nothing here but its
              membership — no transform, no style — so one that holds a single shape, or whose whole
              content is one other group, says exactly as much as no group at all. Delete members
              until one is left and the group dissolves around it, and an imported
              <g> wrapping only another <g> collapses into it.
            
            • 
          
          

          
          Clipboard
          
          
            
            
          • 
              Ctrl+C / X / V copy, cut and paste shapes
              (offset each time). Paste also accepts SVG markup copied from anywhere, and works
              between browser tabs. Ctrl+D duplicates.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Duplicate is on the bar beside the selection. With nothing selected a small bar
              sits above the tools with Paste and Select everything. Paste takes SVG
              markup copied from another app — your browser may ask first.
            
            • 
          
          

          
          History
          
          
            
            
          • 
              Undo / Redo buttons in the toolbar, or Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y (or
                Ctrl+Shift+Z).
            
            • 
          
          

          
          Document panel
          
          
            
            
          • 
              The Document button (first in the toolbar) toggles a panel docked on the left.
              It floats over the canvas and stays open while you work. Its sections (Documents,
              Reference images, SVG, Primitives) fold open and closed, and are remembered. The panel
              is one scrolling surface: only the SVG source keeps a scrollbar of its own. Every row
              across the lists is built the same way, and the control at its head says which kind of
              choice it is — a round radio where only one thing can be chosen (which document is
              open), a square checkbox where any number can (which shapes are selected). The eye on
              a row shows or hides that shape or reference image.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Lock — the padlock on the bar, or the Locked box in a shape's row, puts a shape
              out of reach on the canvas: it is not clicked, box-selected or selected with
              everything else, so what you trace over it can be drawn without nudging it. Its row
              shows a padlock and still selects it, and the bar then offers to unlock it. A locked
              shape is still snapped to, and is exported like any other.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Reference images — add reference images to trace; each row is the file it came
              from, and clickingtapping
              that file name swaps in a different one. Open the row for position, scale, rotation
              and opacity; the eye shows the overlay or hides it.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              SVG — the artboard size (width × height), the grid (its visibility and its step
              in artboard units), the background colour, and the live markup, which you can
              edit directly: changes apply to the drawing after a short pause (an error
              message appears if the markup is invalid, and nothing is changed until it is valid).
              Click or move the cursor into a shape's line and that shape is selected, exactly as if
              you had picked it in Primitives; selected shapes are shown in blue. When a field in a
              Primitives row has focus, the SVG attribute it edits is highlighted in yellow, even if
              the shape is not selected. Only shapes the app knows (path, line, rect, circle,
              ellipse, polyline, polygon, text) are kept. Icons import, export and copy the SVG
              (coordinates are exported as integers), and export it as a PNG at the artboard's size,
              one pixel to a unit.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Background — the swatch beside the grid paints the whole artboard, alpha
              included. At alpha 0 the document is transparent, shown on the canvas as a
              checkerboard and exported with no background at all; at anything above 0 it is
              exported as a full-artboard <rect id="background">, so what you see
              is what the file contains.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Primitives — one row per shape (see Style). The eye hides a shape: it is not
              drawn and cannot be clicked, but stays in the document, exported with
              display="none" so the file still holds it.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              The first time Vellum opens it shows Vellum Workbench, a workbench drawn in
              isometric view — a desk, a laptop, a mug, a plant. Each is a named group, so the
              Primitives list reads like layers; open one up and pull it apart. Its backdrop is
              see-through, which is why the checkerboard shows. Delete it when you are done; it does
              not come back.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Documents save automatically, like Pages or Notes on macOS — there is no Save button.
              Documents lists everything stored in this browser (reference images included).
              ClickTap a document to
              open it; clicktap the
              name of the open document to rename it in place. Every row reads like the other lists:
              a dot showing which one is open, then duplicate, save to a file, ▲ ▼ to move it up or
              down the list, and delete (deleting an empty document doesn't ask; others do). The
              list keeps the order you give it; saving never reshuffles it. + starts a new
              document at the top (named Untitled, Untitled 2, …; the current one is already saved).
              The last document reopens when you start the app.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Documents live in this browser only, so move them out as files: the ⬆ on a row saves
              that document (reference images included) as Name.vellum.json, and the ⬆
              in the Documents header saves every document in one library file — a backup, or
              everything at once for another browser. The ⬇ beside it opens files of either kind,
              several at a time: each document in them is added beside what you already have, under
              a free name, and never overwrites anything.
                To empty a document, Ctrl+A then Delete.
            
            • 
          
          

          
          Toolbar: grid and view
          
          
            
            
          • 
              The grid-snap switch (a ring on a grid crossing)
                (or G)
              toggles snap to grid; a marker shows where the point will land. Snapping applies to
              drawing, dragged handles and moved shapes; hold Alt to switch to alignment snapping instead. It sits with the drawing tools, because that is what it is for. Whether the grid is
              shown, and how far apart its lines are, belong to the document, so they live in the
              SVG section of the Document panel beside the artboard size.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Guides — drag from the top ruler for a horizontal guide, from the left one for
              a vertical guide. With either snap switch on, points land on a guide they come near
              and a moved shape lines up an edge or its centre with one. Drag a guide to move it;
              drop it back on its ruler, or double-clickdouble-tap it, to take it away. Guides are saved with
              the document and never exported. On a phone, where the rulers are hidden, guides
              already there can still be moved and removed.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Zoom is one control: it reads the current level and opens a list to set it, and
              it follows the view however you changed it — a wheelpinch shows up here too. The list also fits the
              artboard or the selection in view
                (Shift+1 and Shift+2;
                Shift+0 is 100%). Beside it, the eye (Final SVG: hides the grid, guides and reference images).
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Nothing in the bar is behind a menu: Document, undo, redo, zoom, Final SVG and Help
              all fit across the top, and the tools and the snap switches sit at the bottom where
              your thumb is. On a phone the light / dark switch is here, at the top of this panel.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Light / dark — the sun or moon beside Help switches the theme. Until you use
              it, Vellum follows your system's setting; once you have, it keeps your choice, and so
              does every other Workbench tool.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Reloading the page puts you back where you were: the zoom and pan, and whichever
              panels were open, belong to the browser tab rather than to the drawing, and are
              restored with it. A new tab starts fresh.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              On a phone both bars float over the canvas rather than taking a strip of it, so the
              artboard keeps the screen. They start below the ruler, and the canvas between the
              buttons still pans — only the buttons themselves take the touch.
            
            • 
          
          

          
          The bar beside the selection
          
          
            
            
          • 
              Select a shape and a small bar appears next to it with what applies: add to the
              selection, select everything, delete, duplicate, send backward / bring forward (dimmed
              when there is nowhere to go), close or open a path, join two paths, group, merge and
              ungroup, and edit text. It is the same set the keyboard shortcuts reach, within reach
              of a thumb.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Select one point of a path, or grab one of its handles, and the bar moves
              beside that point and narrows to it: make it a curve or a corner, remove the handle
              you grabbed, link or break its handles, split the path here, close or open it, delete
              this point. The whole-shape actions step aside, because beside a highlighted point
              they would act on something other than what is highlighted — delete most of all.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              While the pen has a path open the bar shows that instead: take back the last point,
              close the path and finish, just finish, or throw the path away. That is how you end a
              path without a keyboard.
            
            • 
          
          

          
          Rulers
          
          
            
            
          • 
              Rulers along the top and left show artboard units and a cursor marker. When the
              Document panel is open they start to its right.
            
            • 
          
          

          
          Touch
          
          
            
            
          • 
              Handles get a finger-sized target on a touch screen, and points that sit close
              together keep smaller ones so neighbours stay reachable. Zoom in where it is tight.
            
            • 
            
          • 
              Any tool can grab an existing handle: with the rectangle or ellipse tool active,
              touching a handle of a shape already on the canvas edits that shape rather than
              starting a new one.
